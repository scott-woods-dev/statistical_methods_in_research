Appendix B — Datasets

Every dataset used in this book is available as a CSV file, with one row per observation and one column per variable. Each section below names the file, describes it, lists its variables in a table, explains how the data were collected, and cites the source. Data files as of November 4, 2026.

Download any file from its section, or get all 22 files and this key together as one ZIP archive.

Description. This dataset contains demographic, economic, and lifestyle information from a sample of individuals living in the United States, drawn from the 2023 American Community Survey.

File. A comma-separated values (.csv) file containing 10,000 observations (rows) and 9 variables (columns).

Download acs_2023.csv (CSV file, 248 KB)

Variables in acs_2023.csv, with data type and coding key.
Variable Name Data Type Description and Coding Keys

Details. The American Community Survey (ACS) is an ongoing demographics survey administered by the United States Census Bureau. Source. United States Census Bureau. (2023). American Community Survey. https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs

B.2 amas_math_anxiety.csv

Description. This dataset contains individual item-level responses measuring mathematics anxiety using the 9-item Abbreviated Mathematics Anxiety Scale (AMAS). The subjects are a large sample of adult workers evaluating their subjective tension and anxiety across various academic and testing scenarios.

File. A comma-separated values (.csv) file containing 2,200 observations (rows) and 9 variables (columns).

Download amas_math_anxiety.csv (CSV file, 39 KB)

Variables in amas_math_anxiety.csv, with data type and coding key.
Variable Name Data Type Description and Coding Keys
item_1 Numerical Subjective anxiety rating when having to use the tables in the back of a math book, recorded on a \(1 \text{ to } 5\) scale where a value of \(1\) indicates low anxiety and \(5\) indicates high anxiety.
item_2 Numerical Subjective anxiety rating when thinking about an upcoming math test one day before, recorded on a \(1 \text{ to } 5\) scale where a value of \(1\) indicates low anxiety and \(5\) indicates high anxiety.
item_3 Numerical Subjective anxiety rating when watching a teacher work an algebraic equation on the blackboard, recorded on a \(1 \text{ to } 5\) scale where a value of \(1\) indicates low anxiety and \(5\) indicates high anxiety.
item_4 Numerical Subjective anxiety rating when taking an examination in a math course, recorded on a \(1 \text{ to } 5\) scale where a value of \(1\) indicates low anxiety and \(5\) indicates high anxiety.
item_5 Numerical Subjective anxiety rating when being given a homework assignment of many difficult problems due the next class meeting, recorded on a \(1 \text{ to } 5\) scale where a value of \(1\) indicates low anxiety and \(5\) indicates high anxiety.
item_6 Numerical Subjective anxiety rating when listening to a lecture in a math class, recorded on a \(1 \text{ to } 5\) scale where a value of \(1\) indicates low anxiety and \(5\) indicates high anxiety.
item_7 Numerical Subjective anxiety rating when listening to another student explain a math formula, recorded on a \(1 \text{ to } 5\) scale where a value of \(1\) indicates low anxiety and \(5\) indicates high anxiety.

item_9 Numerical Subjective anxiety rating when starting a new chapter in a math book, recorded on a \(1 \text{ to } 5\) scale where a value of \(1\) indicates low anxiety and \(5\) indicates high anxiety.

Details. This dataset represents baseline psychological profiling measurements collected as part of a major within-study comparison research project conducted by academic researchers in 2022. A sample of \(n = 2,200\) participants was recruited via the Amazon Mechanical Turk crowdsourcing platform. The primary purpose of the original study was to create a high-quality empirical benchmark database to evaluate how well non-experimental causal effect estimation methods replicate the findings of randomized controlled trials within educational training settings. Before participating in any educational intervention exercises, respondents completed the full 9-item Abbreviated Mathematics Anxiety Scale (AMAS) to capture variations in baseline quantitative anxiety. The AMAS is widely implemented in psychometric literature because it efficiently captures two distinct but correlated dimensions of quantitative stress: math evaluation anxiety (items 2, 4, 5, and 8) and math learning anxiety (items 1, 3, 6, 7, and 9).

Source. Hopko, D. R., Mahadevan, R., Bare, R. L., & Hunt, M. K. (2003). The Abbreviated Math Anxiety Scale (AMAS): Construction, validity, and reliability. Assessment, 10(2), 178–182. https://doi.org/10.1177/1073191103010002008

Keller, B., Park, S., & Zhang, J. (2022). A new four-arm within-study comparison: Design, implementation, and data. Working paper/dataset documentation.

B.8 depression_diet_treatment.csv

Description. This dataset contains results from a randomized controlled experiment tracking the impact of a short-term, three-week healthy diet intervention on depression symptoms and body mass index (BMI) among young adults.

File. A comma-separated values (.csv) file containing 75 observations (rows) and 10 variables (columns).

Download depression_diet_treatment.csv (CSV file, 3 KB)

Variables in depression_diet_treatment.csv, with data type and coding key.
Variable Name Data Type Description and Coding Keys
Group Categorical The experimental group assignment for the student (Control or Diet).
CESD1 Numerical Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale (CESD) score measured at baseline on Day 1. High scores indicate greater depression severity.
CESD21 Numerical Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale (CESD) score measured at the conclusion of the study on Day 21.
CESDDiff Numerical The change in the CESD depression score over the 21 days (\(\text{Day 21} - \text{Day 1}\)). A negative value indicates an improvement in symptoms.
DASS1 Numerical Depression, Anxiety, and Stress Scale (DASS) depression subscale score measured at baseline on Day 1. High scores indicate greater self-reported depression severity.
DASS21 Numerical Depression, Anxiety, and Stress Scale (DASS) depression subscale score measured at the conclusion of the study on Day 21.
DASSDiff Numerical The change in the DASS depression score over the 21 days (\(\text{Day 21} - \text{Day 1}\)). A negative value indicates an improvement in symptoms.
BMI1 Numerical The participant’s Body Mass Index (BMI) measured at baseline on Day 1.
BMI21 Numerical The participant’s Body Mass Index (BMI) measured at the conclusion of the study on Day 21.
BMIDiff Numerical The change in the participant’s Body Mass Index (BMI) over the 21 days (\(\text{Day 21} - \text{Day 1}\)).

Details. A group of researchers in Australia conducted a short, three-week dietary intervention using a randomized controlled experimental design. The study sample consisted of 75 college-age students who initially exhibited both elevated depression symptoms and relatively poor dietary habits. Participants were randomly assigned to either a healthy diet intervention group (which received healthy food hampers and dietary guidance) or a control group (which maintained their normal eating habits).

To track changes in mental health, the researchers recorded scores across two distinct numerical depression metrics at the start and end of the 21-day period: the CESD scale, which leans more heavily toward clinical observations of depression, and the DASS depression subscale, which relies primarily on subjective, self-reported symptom frequency. Physical changes were also monitored by calculating each participant’s Body Mass Index (BMI) on Day 1 and Day 21.

Source. Francis, H. M., Stevenson, R. J., Chambers, J. R., Joint, D., Dickie, E. W., & Burger, C. (2019). A brief diet intervention can reduce symptoms of depression in young adults: A randomised controlled trial. PLoS ONE, 14(10), e0222768. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0222768
