**Statistical Methods in Research: A Second Course in Statistics**  
**Unit I: Foundations and Data Integrity**  
**Chapter 1: Review: Hypothesis Testing and Effect Sizes**  
- Review of Single and Two-Sample *t*-tests.  
- Effect Sizes as Post Hoc Analysis: Cohen's *d*, Hedges'  *g*.  
- Confidence Intervals for Differences in Means.  
- Nonparametric Alternatives to t-tests.  
**Chapter 2: Formulating Research Questions**  
- From a Research Question to a Statistical Question.  
- Using variables of interest to decide parameters.  
- Using parameters to choose hypotheses.  
- Choosing a statistical test based on hypotheses.  
**Chapter 3: Data Visualization**  
- Principles of Effective Data Visualization.  
- Visualizing Categorical Data (Bar plots, Pareto charts, Pie charts).  
- Visualizing Quantitative Data (Histograms, Box plots, Scatter plots).  
- Detecting Model Violations through Visual Diagnostics.  
**Unit II: Linear Regression and Transformations**  
**Chapter 4: Simple Linear Regression Revisited**  
- **Foundations of Modeling**: Transitioning from 101 concepts to advanced modeling.  
- **Conditions for Linear Models**: Assessing linearity, independence, and homoscedasticity through residual analysis.  
- **Interpreting Regression Slope**: Connecting coefficients to real-world economic and psychological outcomes.  
- **Data Cleaning**: Handling outliers and influential points.  
**Chapter 5: Breaking the Linear Barrier**  
- **Conceptual Introduction to Logarithms**:  
- Understanding logs as a "scaling" tool for skewed social science data.  
- Algebraic review: Working with exponential and logarithmic functions.  
- **Data Transformations**:  
- Re-expressing variables to meet model conditions.  
- Choosing the right transformation for the response (*y*) or predictor ( *x*).  
**Chapter 6: Multiple Regression for Complex Systems**  
- **Adding Predictors**: Using multiple variables to account for confounding factors in social research.  
- **Predicting Outcomes**: Application in Economics (e.g., housing prices based on location, size, and amenities).  
- **Categorical Predictors**: Integrating binary variables (like treatment vs. control) into regression models.  
- **Interactions and Multicollinearity**: Understanding how social variables influence one another.  
**Unit III: Design of Experiments (ANOVA)**  
**Chapter 7: One-Way ANOVA and Randomized Experiments**  
- **Principles of Design**: Randomization, replication, and control in psychological studies.  
- **Fitting the One-Way Model**: Assessing differences between multiple treatment groups.  
- **Post Hoc Analysis**: Multiple comparisons using Fisher’s Least Significant Difference (LSD).  
- **Kruskal-Wallis Test**: A nonparametric alternative for comparing multiple groups.  
**Chapter 8: Advanced Experimental Designs**  
- **Blocking**: Reducing variation by grouping subjects with similar characteristics.  
- **Two-Way ANOVA**:  
- Analyzing interactions between two categorical factors.  
- Application in Education/Social Science: Assessing the combined impact of teaching methods and student backgrounds.  
- **Assessing Normality**: Validating the underlying assumptions of ANOVA.  
- **Fixed vs. Random Effects**: Understanding when factor levels are fixed choices vs. a random sample.  
- **Nesting and Hierarchy**: Designs where levels of one factor are unique to a level of another factor.  
**Unit IV: Categorical Data Analysis**  
**Chapter 9: Analyzing Relationships in Categorical Data**  
- **Chi-Square Tests**:  
- The test for association and independence.  
- Goodness-of-Fit tests for checking distribution patterns.  
- Fisher's Exact Test for small sample sizes.  
- **Measuring Dependence**: Understanding the relationship between discrete social variables.  
**Chapter 10: Logistic Regression**  
- **Introduction to Binary Outcomes**: Modeling yes/no results (e.g., student graduation, fidelity studies).  
- **The Logit Link**: Using logs to connect categorical outcomes to quantitative predictors.  
- **Odds Ratios**: Interpreting the likelihood of an event occurring in social science contexts.  
- **Assessing Fit**: Evaluating the accuracy of logistic models for decision-making.  
**Chapter 11: Ethics in Statistics and Data Integrity**  
- Privacy concerns and data security in human subjects research.  
- Bias in algorithms and the promotion of transparency.  
- The Four-Step Modeling Process: Choose a form, fit to data, assess accuracy, and use for inference.
